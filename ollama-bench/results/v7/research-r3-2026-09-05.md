# v7 round-3 research: ten new task ideas for the traversal suite
*Renamed 2026-09-06 from `v7/research-r3-2026-09-08.md`: the campaign labelled rounds by planned campaign day, not by the calendar date they were written.*

*Written by an Opus research worker for the control session, campaign date 2026-09-08. Read-only
pass over `public-benchmarks-2026-09-05.md`, `plan-r3-2026-09-06.md` §2-4, `authoring-2026-09-06.md`
§8, `org/bench-v4-carryover-2026-09-03.md` and `authoring/r2/SPEC.md`, then web research for
sources **not** already in the public-benchmarks References. No GPU was used, no model was run and
no other file was written.*

Framing: the accepted suite is saturated (Sonnet 5 20/20, GLM 5.3 Flash 20/20, Luna 19/20, Haiku
4.5 18/20, IQ2_M 19/20). The nine round-two candidates all share **one** structural property —
"the answer is a set or a value computed over every stage in the tree, and membership turns on a
comparison between two artifact kinds" (`authoring-2026-09-06.md` §3). That property solves
coverage; it does not, on its own, solve **variety**, and a suite of nine variants of one predicate
is one bet, not nine. Everything below is chosen to add a second, third and fourth predicate shape
on top of that base.

---

## 1. What the new sources say separates Haiku-class from Sonnet-class models

**1.1 The gap is rule *application*, not rule *retrieval* — and extra tokens do not close it.**
HANDBOOK.md (long-context agentic instruction following, 20-124 page binding policy documents,
824 deterministic criteria) reports frontier strict pass@1 at 21.5-36.2% against a mid-tier band of
5-16%, and attributes the gap to application rather than missing facts: efficiency-tier
configurations burn 45-55K tokens per trial to score single digits, against 13-60K for frontier.
Its second named failure mode is literally *"check runs; result ignored"* — the agent retrieves the
governing datum and then acts against it. [S1]

**1.2 Mid-tier degrades on aggregation about twice as fast as frontier does.** Oolong scores
atomic-chunk analysis followed by counting, distributional and temporal aggregation. At 55K tokens
the best model scores 0.601 and mid-range GPT-5-mini 0.499; at 175K they are 0.479 and 0.239. The
frontier row loses ~0.12; the mid-tier row loses ~0.26 over the same stretch. Aggregation over many
units, at length, is the single cleanest published tier separator I found. [S2]

**1.3 Hop depth separates tiers gently and length separates them hard.** Multi-hop long-context
evaluation reports ~12 points lost from 1-hop to 4-hop at 64k, and 4-6 points from 64k to 128k on
1-hop, with the diagnosis that models *retrieve the right passages and fail to chain them*. Depth
alone is a weak lever; depth **at** length is the lever. [S3]

**1.4 Weaker models commit early and then defend the commitment.** Premature commitment is
measurable as cross-run hidden-state convergence at an early step; trajectory-consistent runs are
32-55 points more accurate on HotpotQA than inconsistent ones, and an evidence-conditioned
execution layer that simply postpones actions whose preconditions are unmet lifts SWE-bench
Verified pass@1 by 4.8-11.8 points with no model change. A task whose first plausible reading is
complete and wrong is therefore a tier discriminator by construction. [S4]

**1.5 Prohibitions survive; obligations rot.** Constraint-decay work finds omission-type
constraints ("always also do X") decay markedly with accumulated history while commission-type
prohibitions ("never do Y") persist; a companion result measures in-context governance violation
rising from 0% with the policy fully visible to 30% after compaction and 59% for some models, with
soft organisation-specific policy decaying +50 points against +6 for hard safety norms. The suite's
existing scope gates test the *durable* half of that pair. [S5][S6]

**1.6 Lexical search succeeds exactly where an identifier is explicit, and fails on implicit
dependency.** Grep-style repository retrieval is strong on class-name (35.96%), method-name
(41.47%) and variable-name (18.37%) patterns and fails on generic keywords ("init", "config") and
on relationships with no lexical surface. That is the mechanical statement of rung 0: an answer
keyed on an identifier is greppable, and an answer keyed on something that must first be
*computed* is not. [S7]

**1.7 Distractors and low prompt-to-answer lexical similarity accelerate degradation
non-uniformly.** Chroma's context-rot study over 18 models finds performance falls faster with
input length as needle-question similarity falls, that a single distractor measurably lowers
accuracy with the effect amplifying at length, and — counter-intuitively — that models do better
on shuffled haystacks than logically structured ones. Coherent, well-organised material is
therefore *harder*, which is exactly what this suite already generates. [S8]

**1.8 Frontier failures concentrate in verification, so verification cannot be the whole task.**
The Terminal-Bench task-design guideline reports the Verification class at 47-60% of failures for
frontier models. A task that turns entirely on "did you check before claiming" will fail Sonnet
too, and under `plan-r3-2026-09-06.md` §5.1 a task Sonnet fails is presumed unfair. [S9]

**1.9 Abstention does not scale and is a poor primary predicate.** AbstentionBench finds abstention
unsolved and scarcely improved by scale; AgentAbstain's paired-twin design (each task ships a twin
differing by one detail, so "always act" and "always refuse" both cap at 50%) puts the best model at
59.5% paired accuracy, mean 45.7%, with act accuracy 80.6% against abstain 59.1%. Useful as a
*subcheck*; unfair as a whole task. [S10][S11]

**1.10 Aggressive quantisation lengthens reasoning before it breaks accuracy, and arithmetic is
where it breaks first.** 3-bit AWQ takes MATH-500 from 85.6% to 47.0% while average chain-of-thought
grows 5.2K → 23.4K tokens (4.5x), with the effect strongest for smaller models and harder
benchmarks; 2-bit degradation is reported as a property of model-benchmark *pairs*, not of
benchmarks. For the IQ2_M workhorse this predicts that a task with a genuine arithmetic aggregate
over the tree will cost it turns and output budget before it costs it accuracy — which is a reason
to keep the arithmetic real but small (sums of a dozen integers, not a dozen divisions). [S12]

**1.11 Long-horizon difficulty converges the tiers if pushed too far.** The long-horizon failure
taxonomy over 3,100+ trajectories reports process-level risks at 72.5% of failures against 27.5%
design-level, and explicitly that model gaps *narrow* once tasks enter the breaking region. There is
a window: hard enough that Haiku falls, not so hard that Sonnet joins it. [S13]

## 2. Ten task ideas

Numbering `n01`-`n10`. `n01`-`n07` are main band (48-64k, 29,000-36,000 tokens of material per
SPEC.md); `n08`-`n10` are cheap band (24k). Every idea inherits the round-two base property
(the answer is computed over the whole tree) and adds a **second** predicate that the nine round-two
slots do not have. "Hops" is distinct causal hops for `load_bearing()`; SPEC.md's floor is 6 paths
and 3 hops, acceptance touch is 5.

**The index-leak guard, stated once and binding on all ten.** `authoring-2026-09-06.md` §8 item 2
found that `make_corpus.py` writes each stage's `limit` and `window_s` into five agreeing artifacts,
two of which enumerate every stage in one small file, and that seven of nine candidates had built
their predicate on exactly that pair. So for every idea below, the decisive per-unit datum is one
the generator does **not** replicate: it is written by `overlay()` into exactly one artifact per
unit, of a kind that no summary file carries, and `r2/check_index_leak.py` must be re-run and must
find no file naming more than one unit's decisive datum. Where an idea needs a roster of units at
all, that roster is declared `"named_in_prompt": True` on the manifest entry — knowing the scope of
a sweep is not knowing its answer — and nothing else is.

---

### n01 — the obligation nobody asked for (main band)

- **Axis:** *omission-constraint retention.* New. The nine round-two slots gate on commission
  (`SCOPE_GATE`, `MUST_NOT_EXIST`: do not create, do not touch). This is the inverse and, per [S5],
  the half that actually decays.
- **Material:** a release-engineering tree. Each stage carries a class marker in its own module. A
  standing rule, in one prose operations record, says that any stage of class B additionally
  requires a *derived* countersign line appended to that stage's own record, computed from two of
  its fields. Three of ~14 stages are class B; class is not in any summary artifact.
- **Predicate:** deliverable answers the asked question (the aggregate over all stages) **and**
  `editable()` covers the three class-B records, whose final bytes are compared to the reference. A
  run that answers the asked question perfectly and appends nothing scores the asked keys and loses
  the obligation group.
- **Hops:** 7 load-bearing paths, 4 hops — `class` (per-stage module), `rule` (prose record),
  `formula` (countersign derivation), `enumeration` (the stage records themselves).
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** [S1]'s failure modes 2 and 3 and [S5]'s omission decay both predict a
  mid-tier model reads the rule, does the asked work, and never returns to the rule after ~10 tool
  calls. Sonnet's observed behaviour on HANDBOOK-shaped material is to re-derive the standing rule
  before finishing. Fair because the rule is stated once, plainly, in ordinary prose.
- **SPEC fit:** `overlay()` writes the rule and class markers; `facts()` groups the asked keys and
  the obligation separately (SPEC.md: never put two independently-winnable facts in one group);
  `editable()` for the three records; `probes()` case 3 = "asked question right, obligation absent"
  → `confidently_wrong` at the partial score.

### n02 — supersession, ordered by date and not by position (main band)

- **Axis:** *temporal supersession with a position decoy.* New. `m09-main-luna` has a *governing
  amendment* as one key; it does not have several live amendments per unit whose ordering is the
  whole predicate.
- **Material:** a policy-records directory of dated amendment files plus per-stage modules. Several
  stages carry two or three amendments. The **latest-dated** amendment sits early in every listing
  order (lexical filename order, directory order and the README's own reading order all put the
  superseded one last), so "last file wins" is wrong for exactly the stages that matter.
- **Predicate:** the set of stages whose effective value comes from an amendment that is *not* the
  last one a directory listing shows, plus the resulting total. Not answerable from any index: the
  dates live in the amendment bodies, one per file, and no artifact lists them together.
- **Hops:** 9 paths, 5 hops — `date`, `ruling`, `definition`, `enumeration`, `arithmetic`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** Oolong's temporal-aggregation family is where mid-tier loses most [S2], and
  [S4]'s premature commitment is directly baited: the first complete reading (take the last file) is
  self-consistent and wrong. Sonnet passes because sorting a dozen ISO dates is not hard once you
  decide to look at them; the task is fair since the dates are unambiguous and stated in every file.
- **SPEC fit:** `kinds` = `set` for the stage set, `int` for the total; `sweep_paths()` naturally
  covers every amendment plus every stage module, which is where the 60-80% coverage comes from.

### n03 — three-way disagreement with the tiebreak in prose (main band)

- **Axis:** *source-of-truth precedence over conflicting evidence.* New in kind.
  `m05-main-claude` is a binary doc-vs-module reconciliation; this is a three-way disagreement whose
  resolution rule is itself buried, and it is the axis `bench-v4-carryover-2026-09-03.md` §2 names
  as the one every model fails: *"the parts of a spec stated only in prose and never shown in an
  example"*, with precedence the sharpest instance.
- **Material:** for each stage, three artifact kinds disagree in a rotating pattern — a markdown
  configuration table, a Python module constant, and a CSV migration row. One prose paragraph in an
  engineering record fixes the precedence between the three kinds, and it is **not** the obvious
  ordering (the CSV outranks the module for one named property only).
- **Predicate:** per-stage effective value under the stated precedence, aggregated; plus the key
  naming the artifact kind that governs the exception. ConfRAG's finding is that models detect the
  conflict and then fail to *organise* it [S14]; this grades the organising rule.
- **Hops:** 10 paths, 5 hops — `precedence` (the prose), `three enumerations` (one per artifact
  kind), `exception`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** a mid-tier model resolves conflicts by recency-in-context or by artifact
  plausibility rather than by the stated rule; v4 measured `55_minilang`'s prose-only precedence
  check failing in *every* Haiku trial. Sonnet lost one or two checks on that axis, never the task,
  so the fairness margin is measured rather than assumed.
- **SPEC fit:** one `groups` entry per independent fact; `probes()` gets two wrong-but-plausible
  cases, one per plausible alternative precedence, each landing on its own partial score.

### n04 — replay the log, do not read the state (main band)

- **Axis:** *state tracking over an ordered event log.* New. All nine round-two slots read a
  *current* value from an artifact; none replays a sequence. τ²-bench isolates exactly this as
  long-horizon state tracking under partial observability [S15], and BABILong's fact-chaining and
  list/set families are the long-context version [S16].
- **Material:** an append-only `ops/journal/*.tsv` split over ~10 files, carrying `apply`,
  `revert`, `reapply` and `rescind` events against per-stage settings. The per-stage modules carry
  the **initial** values only, and are honest about it. No file holds the final state; it exists
  only as a replay.
- **Predicate:** the final effective value per stage after replaying the journal in event order
  (not file order — events carry sequence numbers and the files interleave), the set of stages whose
  final value differs from its initial one, and the sequence number of the last event that changed
  anything. A `revert` of a `revert` is present, and one `rescind` targets an event that never
  applied — a no-op a careless replay double-counts.
- **Hops:** 12 paths, 5 hops — `initial`, `event`, `ordering`, `semantics` (what `rescind` means,
  defined once in prose), `enumeration`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** [S3]'s "retrieve the right passages, fail to chain them" is the whole
  failure; and per [S12] the quant will spend turns re-deriving. Sonnet passes because each
  individual event is unambiguous — the difficulty is bookkeeping over ~40 events, which is
  conceptual-but-mechanical difficulty, not a trick, and so passes [S9]'s legibility test.
- **SPEC fit:** `facts()` must compute the replay from `seed/` on disk with its own asserts (SPEC.md
  is explicit that a reference asserting a fact the material does not state is this benchmark's most
  expensive defect, twice occurred). `RUN_TESTS` unused; `SCOPE_GATE = True`.

### n05 — the join key must be computed before it can be searched (main band)

- **Axis:** *non-lexical join.* This is a deliberate second slot on `m09`'s semantic-bridge axis,
  and it is worth one: `m09` bridges word → word → word, which [S7] shows is still lexical
  retrieval with extra steps, whereas here the bridge is **arithmetic**, so no grep exists at any
  hop, not even a clever one.
- **Material:** stages are addressed in documents by a human label, in modules by an opaque
  four-digit code, and the mapping between them is not tabulated anywhere: the code is defined by a
  rule ("the code is the deployment ordinal times the region offset, both of which are stated
  per-stage") given once in prose, with the two inputs in two different artifact kinds.
- **Predicate:** the answer names stages by label but can only be gathered by code, so the solver
  must compute ~14 codes before it can read anything. Nothing an index could hold: the codes exist
  nowhere on disk.
- **Hops:** 8 paths, 4 hops — `rule`, `ordinal`, `offset`, `enumeration`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** [S7] is the direct evidence — lexical retrieval carries mid-tier agents
  through repository tasks, and this removes it entirely; the observed mid-tier fallback is to
  answer from the labels alone. Fair: the arithmetic is one multiplication per stage and the rule is
  stated without ambiguity. Also the cleanest possible rung-0 candidate, since `check_rung0.py`'s
  selective-grep test cannot even be baited.
- **SPEC fit:** `prompt()` uses labels only and never a code, so it shares no token with the
  load-bearing modules; `load_bearing()` marks the label roster `"named_in_prompt": True` and
  nothing else.

### n06 — the same quantity in four units (main band)

- **Axis:** *silent unit and scale coercion inside an aggregate.* New. `m03` and `m09` sum limits;
  neither makes the summands incommensurable.
- **Material:** a per-stage budget expressed as `ms` in modules, `s` in the operations docs, `MiB`
  in one CSV and `MB` in another, with the unit written in the field name in some files and in a
  neighbouring comment in others. One conversion factor (the base the project treats as canonical)
  is fixed in a single prose line and is *not* the SI default.
- **Predicate:** the normalised total and the set of stages whose two declarations disagree once
  normalised. SciTrek and Oolong both isolate counting/filtering/aggregating/sorting over
  multi-document material as the family where long-context models drop most [S2][S17].
- **Hops:** 9 paths, 4 hops — `canonical base` (prose), `declaration` × 2 kinds, `enumeration`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** mid-tier adds the numbers as printed. Per [S12] this is also where the 2-bit
  workhorse should cost itself the most output budget, which is the desired direction for the ~50%
  target. Kept fair by using integers and a single conversion per value — [S9]'s warning against
  clerical difficulty means the arithmetic must be trivially correct once the units are right.
- **SPEC fit:** `kinds` = `int` so `1,234` and `1234` agree; `probes()` case 3 = the un-normalised
  total → `confidently_wrong`, which is the exact near-miss that makes the score legible.

### n07 — the tool's output overrules the document (main band)

- **Axis:** *acting on a check whose result contradicts the written expectation.* Distinct from
  `m04-main-glm` (mode 4), which grades *that the checks pass*; this grades *that the checker's
  output beat the documentation*, HANDBOOK.md's failure mode 2, "check runs; result ignored" [S1].
- **Material:** a seed-resident, write-nothing `tools/report_state.py` that prints a per-stage
  derived table. The docs state an expected table that is right for all but three stages. The
  runner's output is authoritative and says so in its own header; the docs do not know they are
  stale.
- **Predicate:** the deliverable carries the three divergent stages plus a value only the runner
  emits. Because `RUN_TESTS` re-runs the runner at grade time and compares, a solver that
  transcribed the docs is caught by construction, and grading twice answers the same thing
  (SPEC.md's requirement, and `plan-r3-2026-09-06.md` §4.1's idempotence rule).
- **Hops:** 7 paths, 4 hops — `runner`, `authority` (the header sentence), `expectation` (docs),
  `enumeration`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** [S1] measures mid-tier agents retrieving the governing datum and then acting
  against it; [S4] adds that once the docs are read first, the reading is defended. Fairness margin:
  the task is *not* primarily a verification task — verification is 47-60% of frontier failure
  [S9] — the model is told to run the tool; what is graded is whether it believes it.
- **SPEC fit:** `RUN_TESTS = {"args": ["tools/report_state.py"], ...}`; `probes()` case 4 (an answer
  correct on the task that also did the forbidden thing, here rewriting the stale doc) →
  `unverified_claim`, per SPEC.md's mode-4 requirement.

### n08 — enumerate what is missing (cheap band, 24k)

- **Axis:** *absence as evidence.* New, and structurally impossible to grep: no search returns the
  files that do not contain a thing. Grounded in [S7] (lexical retrieval has no operator for
  absence) and [S10] (models are poor at "the correct answer is nothing here").
- **Material:** ~40 files, ~14-15k tokens — the brief's cheap-band size, which is **2.5-3x the
  current cheap trees**. Calibration recorded the existing cheap band running at 51-335% of its
  material because the conversation outgrows a 4-6k tree; that is why the cheap band saturates, and
  a new cheap task authored at 4-6k will saturate too. Each unit is supposed to carry a signed
  acceptance stub; four do not.
- **Predicate:** the set of units lacking the stub, and the count. Note the two honest traps: one
  unit has a stub that is present but unsigned (present-but-invalid ≠ missing), and one has the stub
  in a differently-named file the prose sanctions.
- **Hops:** 6 paths, 3 hops — `requirement` (prose), `validity` (what signed means), `enumeration`.
  This is the floor SPEC.md allows and it is honest at cheap-band size.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** a mid-tier model greps for the stub marker, gets a list of hits, and reports
  the complement of *the hits it saw* rather than the complement of *the roster* — an off-by-file
  error that grows with tree size. Sonnet enumerates the roster first. Fair: a complete, explicit
  roster exists and is `"named_in_prompt": True`.
- **SPEC fit:** `kinds` = `set` and `int`; `probes()` case 3 = "counts the unsigned stub as
  present", case 3b = "counts the sanctioned alternate name as missing" — two wrong-but-plausible
  answers on different scores, which is what SPEC.md asks for.

### n09 — the first complete answer is wrong (cheap band, 24k)

- **Axis:** *premature commitment.* New. The round-two slots are hard to *start*; this one is easy
  to start and hard to *finish*, which is the failure [S4] measures directly.
- **Material:** ~40 files. The natural first stop — the file whose name matches the prompt's own
  noun — contains a complete, internally consistent, correctly-formatted answer to every key. A
  single-line correction in an unrelated late artifact (a changelog entry, a test fixture comment)
  invalidates exactly one field of it, which changes two of the four keys.
- **Predicate:** the corrected values. The decoy is not a trap in the pejorative sense: it is a
  stale artifact of exactly the kind HANDBOOK.md's "superseded SOP" distractors are [S1], and the
  correction is dated and unambiguous.
- **Hops:** 6 paths, 3 hops — `draft answer`, `correction`, `dependents` (the two keys that move).
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** [S4]'s core result — behavioural divergence concentrates at early decision
  points and trajectory-consistent runs are 32-55 points better. A model that stops at the first
  complete answer scores 2/4 groups; a model that keeps reading scores 4/4. The partial score makes
  this the most *legible* row in the suite. Fair: Sonnet's cheap-band behaviour is to sweep before
  answering, and nothing is hidden.
- **SPEC fit:** the decoy file is load-bearing and declared (it holds three of the four correct
  values); `probes()` case 3 = the decoy verbatim → `confidently_wrong` at exactly 2/4.

### n10 — precedence between failure kinds, stated only in prose (cheap band, 24k)

- **Axis:** *output ordering fixed by a prose-only rule.* A second slot on the precedence axis of
  `n03`, and it is worth it: `bench-v4-carryover-2026-09-03.md` §2 names this as **the one axis
  every model failed in v4** — Haiku, Sonnet, Luna and fp8 alike — and records that one prose-only
  precedence sentence (`55_minilang`: "parse-time errors beat runtime errors, in the stated order")
  decided the whole task in every trial. It has never since been authored on purpose. `n03` applies
  precedence to *sources*; `n10` applies it to *output order*, which is a different mechanism and
  the one v4 measured.
- **Material:** ~40 files, ~14-15k tokens. A small validator's rule set spread over per-rule
  modules, each defining one diagnostic kind, plus a prose specification paragraph fixing the
  precedence *between kinds* (and, within a kind, ordering by position). Several inputs trip more
  than one rule.
- **Predicate:** the deliverable is an **ordered** list of diagnostics for a set of given inputs.
  `kinds = "list"` (order significant) for that key, `set` for a second key, so a solver that finds
  every diagnostic and orders them wrongly loses the ordered group and keeps the set group — the
  gradient v4 lacked.
- **Hops:** 7 paths, 4 hops — `precedence` (prose), `kind definitions`, `inputs`, `positions`.
- **Haiku vs Sonnet:** v4's measurement is the evidence: Haiku lost ten checks on this axis, Sonnet
  one or two, Luna between. Splitting the set from the order converts that magnitude difference into
  a pass/fail boundary that lands between the two tiers instead of below both. Fairness is the
  reason for the split — v4's version failed Sonnet too, and an unsplit version would again.
- **SPEC fit:** `groups` separates "which diagnostics" from "in what order"; `probes()` case 3 =
  correct set, position-only ordering → `confidently_wrong` at the set score; the five perturbations
  are unaffected because ordering is inside a line-sequence, not whitespace.

**Coverage of the ten against the four failure predicates.** Obligation (n01); temporal ordering
(n02, and n04's event order); precedence (n03, n10); state replay (n04); non-lexical join (n05);
unit normalisation (n06); tool-output authority (n07); absence (n08); commitment (n09). Nine
distinct predicates over ten slots, against one predicate over the current nine.

## 3. What not to do, from the sources

1. **Do not build a task whose whole predicate is "verify before claiming."** Verification is
   47-60% of *frontier* failures [S9]; such a task fails Sonnet and is then unfair by
   `plan-r3-2026-09-06.md` §5.1. Make verification a tiebreak subcheck (n07), never the task.
2. **Do not source difficulty from format, permissions or resource starvation** — "real difficulty
   is conceptual rather than environmental," and grade outcomes, not implementations; a task that
   fails on a dollar sign measures format compliance [S9]. This also means keeping n06's arithmetic
   trivially checkable once the units are right.
3. **Do not make abstention or judgement the primary answer.** Best paired accuracy is 59.5% and
   scale does not help [S10][S11]; keep every "nothing qualifies" answer *computable* from a
   declared roster, as n08 does.
4. **Do not exceed about four causal hops.** Model gaps narrow once tasks enter the breaking region
   [S13] and hop depth alone buys only ~12 points at 64k [S3]; depth at length discriminates, depth
   alone converges the tiers at the bottom.
5. **Do not let any artifact enumerate the decisive datum for more than one unit, and do not share
   a single identifier between the prompt and a load-bearing file** — that pair is the round-two
   index leak [`authoring-2026-09-06.md` §8] and is exactly where grep-style retrieval carries a
   mid-tier model through [S7]; re-run `check_index_leak.py` and `check_rung0.py` on all ten.
