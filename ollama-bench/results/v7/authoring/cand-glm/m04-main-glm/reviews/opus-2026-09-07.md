## 3. The one concrete fix

Stop the seed from containing an evaluable copy of the rule, and stop the check from being
searchable in 19 trials. Concretely:

1. `tools/run_checks.py` performs **no sweep**. It reads `data/regression-fixture.csv`,
   normalises it as it does today, and compares one `sha256` against a single hard-coded
   `EXPECTED_DIGEST`, printing only `OK`/`FAIL`. Reading the file then yields no stage name, no
   date, and no rule to evaluate.
2. Widen the fixture so the digest is not brute-forceable. A digest over just the 18 included
   names is **not** sufficient -- 2^19 subsets is one `python -c` away, and the 2b search already
   proves a solver will use `run_checks` as an oracle. Require the fixture to carry all 19
   stages with `stage,declared_verified_on,rollout_verified_on,included`. The search space
   becomes 38 dates rather than 19 bits, which is infeasible, and the 19x2 sweep becomes the only
   route.
3. `tools/build_fixture.py` takes the same widened review, so its date columns become
   load-bearing rather than a boolean.
4. Rewrite `docs/workflows/release-checks.md:35-37` to say what the check actually verifies.

This keeps mode 4 intact: the solver still runs a real check locally and still observes a real
`OK`/`FAIL`, and the grader still re-runs it and still overrides a fabricated `pass` to
`unverified_claim`.
