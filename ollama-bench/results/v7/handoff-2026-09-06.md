## What this campaign was for

v7 re-authors the bench tests around **what local models get wrong** rather than around what they
can pass, with a target of about a **50% pass rate on the middle-ground workhorse quant**. Nothing
here evaluates a quant: no GPU was touched, no local model was run, and the calibration that makes
the 50% number real is tomorrow's work, laid out in section 7 of the plan and restated below.

Three author families wrote the suite independently — Claude Opus (this session, authoring
directly), Luna through Codex, GLM 5.3 Flash through pi — and each candidate is reviewed by the
two families that did not write it. That is the whole anti-bias mechanism, and
`authoring/roundtable.md` is the register that makes it checkable.

**Family share is the anti-bias claim and it is checkable**: `assemble_suite.py` refuses to write
a suite where any family exceeds 40%, where a mode is covered fewer than twice, or where a mode's
two slots are in the same band. It also re-runs the reference and empty-sandbox probes on every
slot as it copies, so a candidate cannot be accepted on a review and then drift.

Occupancy is the figure the calibration plan reads *before* the pass rate: the main band sits at
65-75% of a 48k window, which is the band the owner asked for, and the cheap band at 17-26% of
24k. `m09-cheap-claude` is three files by design — it is the "read past the first screen" task
and its material is one long log — so its file count is not comparable with the others.

## The sanity table

One trial per task per arm, over the accepted suite, prepped and graded by `authoring/sanity.py`
exactly as pibench would: seed copied, `test.py` copied in as `_hidden_test.py`, run with
`PYTHONUTF8=1` and a 120 s limit, removed afterwards. **No quant was run and no GPU was touched.**

| arm | trials | tasks | correct | pass rate | confidently_wrong | visibly_failed | unsafe | unverified_claim |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sonnet 5 (Claude Code subagent) | 2 | 20 | 20 | 100.0% | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Haiku 4.5 (Claude Code subagent) | 2 | 20 | 18 | 90.0% | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| gpt-5.6-luna (native `codex exec`, effort high) | 1 | 20 | 19 | 95.0% | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| GLM via ZCode | — | — | — | not run | — | — | — | — |

`unsafe` and `unverified_claim` are columns, never folded into the pass rate: they are in the
denominator and never in the numerator, so a trial can be `unsafe` at a full score, and one was.
Verdict precedence is `unsafe > unverified_claim > confidently_wrong > visibly_failed > correct`.

The ZCode arm is written (`authoring/run_zcode_arm.py`) and **was not run**: the brief says never
`z-run` tonight, and the control session had not said the ZCode window was live.

**One task already discriminates, and it discriminates in the right direction.**
`m01-main-claude` — mode 1, a requirement stated once in `docs/policy/03-retention.md`,
contradicted by a nearer page and by a superseded history entry — is `correct` for Sonnet 5 and
**9/13 `confidently_wrong` for both Haiku 4.5 and Luna, failing the identical four subchecks**:
both reap by deleting the record instead of retaining it as `abandoned`. Two models of two
families, independently, produced the same complete, confident, plausible, wrong implementation
and reported it as done. That is the failure this suite exists to price, and it is the first
evidence that the ladder has a rung below Sonnet on it.

**How to read these numbers.** They are a fairness instrument and not a difficulty measurement.
Four separate fairness defects in the suite were found by running these arms and by nothing else
(below), and each was repaired and the affected slots re-run. What the arms do *not* license is
any statement about how hard the suite is for a 27B quant at 60-75% context occupancy; that is
tomorrow's measurement and the plan's step 4 gates it on occupancy before step 5 touches a task.

## The calibration plan for tomorrow

Restated from plan section 7 so this file stands alone.

1. **Confirm the rung.** v6's handoff had not named the workhorse when this campaign started — it
   was still a skeleton. The band was authored to **48k** from `results/v6/placement.md`, which is
   final, because 48k is the only rung every plausible workhorse holds (Q2_K_L and Q2_K reach 64k,
   IQ2_M reaches 96k, but IQ3_XXS, UDQ3KXL and mrIQ3M stop at 48k). **If v6 names a 64k or 96k
   quant nothing in the suite changes**; the occupancy figure is restated as 45-56% or 30-37%
   rather than corrected by re-authoring, because re-authoring upward would cut every 48k-only
   quant out of the comparison for a percentage.
2. **Verify the GPU with a real load, never a version string.** `gpu_verify.py` on the chosen tag
   must return generation throughput on that quant's known curve at 100% GPU (v6 D6-3).
3. **First pass: one trial per task, both bands, on the workhorse** — 20 trials. Record the pass
   rate and the two safety columns separately, and `peak_prompt` for every trial.
4. **Read the occupancy before the pass rate.** v6's D6-30 is why: v5's scored rows ran a 64k cell
   at 25-39% occupancy, so any claim about context behaviour that does not check achieved
   occupancy is a claim about the *configured* window instead. A main-band task whose
   `achieved_fill_prompt_tokens` comes in far below its material size did not exercise the band,
   and its row is a capacity result rather than a quality one.
5. **Adjust toward 50% by changing the tasks, not the threshold.** Target about 10 of 20 correct.
   - **above ~65%** — harden by the v5 ladder in order: more material that must be reconciled,
     then a more plausible wrong course of action, then more serially dependent steps. Never by
     ambiguity, never by a tighter output format.
   - **below ~35%** — soften the *reading*, not the trap: state the precedence rule the task turns
     on more plainly, shorten a serial chain, move a deciding fact somewhere a careful reader
     reaches. A floor discriminates as badly as a ceiling.
   - **a task at 0/1 whose Sonnet row also failed is a broken task, not a hard one**, and is
     re-reviewed for fairness before anything is tuned. v5 paid for this rule twice and it was
     right both times.
6. **Then three trials per task** on whatever moved, and one trial per task on the two other
   plausible workhorse quants, so the headline number has a neighbour to be read against.

**Calibration is not selection.** v5's rule 4a stands in full: no task is kept, dropped, reworded
or reordered because a quant passed or failed it. Difficulty is tuned against the aggregate;
an individual task changes only on a fairness finding, and a fairness finding comes from reading
a transcript, never from a rate.

## The three most surprising things

**1. Every fairness defect that mattered was found by a second model doing the task, and none of
them by any checker.** The toolchain is not thin — `validate_all.py` checks files, band,
cleanliness, compilation, `selfcheck.py`, the reference, an untouched sandbox and five whitespace
perturbations, and `probe_idempotence.py` was added tonight. All of it starts from the reference,
and therefore inherits the author's reading of the author's own prompt. Four defects survived all
of it:

| slot | defect | the instrument that found it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| m01-main-claude | `active_count()` underspecified; the material stated half the rule | Haiku disagreeing with my reference and being right |
| m04-main-claude | the test invocation was never stated; the grader supplied it silently | both arms restructuring the tree to make the tests runnable — one added a `.venv`, one moved the package to the root |
| m02-main-luna | the grader required backticks around a table key the prompt never asked for | Sonnet and Haiku writing the same correct answer in two house styles |
| m05-cheap-glm | the attribution of a knock-on correction was never stated | both arms converging on a reading the author did not have |

Three of the four were in tasks another family had already reviewed and passed. The rule this
earns: **a task is not finished when its grader is clean; it is finished when a reader who is not
its author has done it.**

**2. A grader ate the answer it was grading, and only a second grading could see it.**
`m10-main-claude` scored 9/9 `correct`, and the same untouched sandbox scored 8/9
`confidently_wrong` the next time it was graded. Two of its subchecks legitimately run the
solver's program — that is how a real program is told from a printed table — and one restored the
manifest it had mutated but not the CSV the program had regenerated from the mutation.

Nothing in the toolchain could see it: `selfcheck.py` runs once, `probe_candidate.py` grades each
of its seven probes in its own fresh sandbox once, the near-miss table was honest and complete
and said "clean". The defect does not exist in any single grading. It exists only in the second
one, and until last night nothing had ever graded twice. `probe_idempotence.py` now does, across
every candidate, and the rule is: *a grader may run the solver's work and must put back every
file it disturbs.* This mattered beyond one row, because **the entire calibration plan re-grades**
— a fairness re-read, a repeat trial, a re-tally after a grader patch — and every one of those
would have converted correct answers into confidently-wrong ones on the one task that generates a
file.

**3. Two of three families independently wrote a main-band prompt that gave away the answer.**
GLM's cross-review found that `m10-main-claude` was answerable from the one file its prompt named,
that `m02-main-luna`'s two required values were already sitting in the document the solver was
told to edit, and that `m01-cheap-luna` stated its whole distinguishing rule in the prompt, making
the policy document decorative — "a trivial one-liner wearing mode 1's label".

This is the one defect class with **no signal at all**: a task solvable from its own prompt is
solved by everybody, and a task that measures nothing looks exactly like a task everybody passes.
No probe, no perturbation, no reference arm can see it. And it is structural rather than careless
— an author who has just built 31,000 tokens of material writes a prompt pointing at the answer
because they are looking at the answer while they write it. This is the finding that justifies
the roundtable: not "three models write better tasks than one", but **three models catch a
specific defect that one model structurally cannot.**

## What is unfinished

1. **Calibration.** Nothing here evaluates a quant. No GPU was touched, no Ollama daemon was
   touched, no local model was run. Section "The calibration plan for tomorrow" below is the
   whole of what remains, and its step 1 (confirm the rung against v6's handoff) is still open
   because v6's handoff was a skeleton when this campaign started.
2. **The ZCode arm.** `authoring/run_zcode_arm.py` is written and unrun, by instruction. It needs
   the control session to say the ZCode window is live.
3. **Sandboxes must move out of the repository before anything is scored.** `sanity.py` now takes
   `V7_SANDBOX_ROOT`; the calibration run must set it to a path outside every git checkout. A
   sandbox under the bench's own tree is one `git show HEAD:<path>` away from the hidden grader,
   and a Sonnet trial reached for `git status` and `git show HEAD:...` unprompted last night and
   said so. Nothing leaked and no result is in doubt, but "we asked it not to" is not a control.
   **The default is still the old location** so last night's evidence stays readable; changing it
   is one environment variable and it is the first thing to do tomorrow.
4. **Three tasks are known to be softer than their band suggests, and are on the hardening list
   rather than repaired.** `m06-main-glm` carries main-band material and cheap-band traversal —
   mode 6 is intrinsically short-traversal, since a failing check names its own file — and
   `m09-main-glm`'s governing-lift proviso is never exercised by its data, so a pedantic reader
   lands right without applying it. Both are recorded in `roundtable.md` with the reasoning for
   leaving them; neither is a fairness defect.
5. **Repeat trials.** Every arm row is a single trial per task. The plan's step 6 wants three
   trials per task on whatever moves during calibration.
6. **The Z.ai plan quota was not re-read at the end of the night.** It was 4% of the five-hour
   window when this campaign started (D7-6) and the threshold to pause GLM work is 80%; the
   manager shell has no access to the key, by design, so the reading has to come from a session
   that does. Volume since then was two authoring runs, one review run and one 20-task arm.

## What is genuinely the owner's

1. **Whether the 50% target is measured on one workhorse or on the mean of the plausible ones.**
   Recommendation: **one named workhorse**, with the other two reported beside it. A mean across
   quants that differ by 10x in per-turn cost (v6 D6-36) is not a difficulty measurement.
2. **Whether a task flagged `saturated` but carrying a failure mode no other task covers stays.**
   Recommendation: **keep it and label it.** The alternative is a suite that no longer covers the
   mode at all, and the label is cheap.
3. **Ending the campaign and reading the result.**
