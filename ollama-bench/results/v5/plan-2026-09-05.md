# Benchmark plan, 2026-09-05 — desaturation and real context

Written by the control session on the owner's rulings of 2026-09-05. **This file supersedes
`plan-rev5-focused.md` (rev 5.9) wherever the two disagree.** Rev 5.9 stays on disk: sections 1
(mission), 4a (calibration is not selection), 5 (arms and the measured quant curves) and 7
(verdict rule) are still the contract and are referenced rather than copied. The harness, the
grid tags, the checkers and the eight authored tasks all carry forward unchanged;
`harness_version` stays `v5` and results stay non-comparable with v4.

Read `handoff-2026-09-05.md` beside this file first. It carries the state of the machine, the
artifacts, and every trap listed in section 8 with its evidence.

## 0. The three rulings this plan exists to carry

**(a) There is no freeze.** The suite is not frozen, will not be frozen, and no session may block,
wait or ask permission on freezing it. The gate is gone from every document. A benchmark that is
still being sharpened is the normal state of this work, not a defect in it.

**(b) The suite is desaturated before it is scored, to a target of about 80%, and the manager
keeps going after that.** Seven of eight tasks are currently saturated — Haiku passes them 3/3,
including one-shot with no self-checking. That is the finding that matters most from the
authoring campaign, and the answer is not to accept it and rank anyway. The answer is harder
tasks, authored iteratively, measured after each round.

**(c) The synthetic fill is withdrawn.** Context is delivered by material the task genuinely
requires, never by filler injected to hit a token count. The prompt-side fill mechanism built on
2026-09-04 works and is measured, and it is retired anyway: it measures how a model copes with
padding, which is not a question anyone asked. See section 3.

Everything below follows from those three.

## 1. Mission

Unchanged from rev 5.9 section 1, which stands in full: find out whether any real portion of the

still exists when the ChatGPT window is exhausted and weekly-all is spent.

## 2. Desaturation: the target, the ladder, and the guard

### 2.1 The target

**A first-pass target of about 80% on the Haiku row.** Haiku is the discrimination arm: it is the
nearest cloud analogue to a good local 27B, and its rate is what tells us whether the suite can
separate anything. Today it is effectively 100%. Bring it to roughly **19 or 20 of 24 trials**
(eight tasks x three) and the suite has room to rank in the region the local quants will actually
occupy.

**The risk in this number, stated plainly.** Haiku is a proxy for a good local 27B, not a
measurement of one, and no quant has ever been scored on this suite. If the proxy is wrong in the
optimistic direction, a suite tuned to Haiku-80% could put every quant on the floor, and a floor
discriminates as poorly as a ceiling. Two things follow. **Do not desaturate past the target in
one leap** — run a local calibration pass (section 5 step 3) as soon as the suite is re-banded, and
if the best quant is scoring near zero on the sharpened suite, say so and stop hardening. And if
the quants do floor out on a suite a small cloud model handles four times in five, **that is a
result and it is reported as one**: it is a direct answer to the mission's question, not a failure
of the benchmark.

Rev 5.9 section 4's older rule — "at most three tasks saturated at 3/3" — is **superseded by this
number**. It was a per-task rule with no aggregate target; this is the aggregate target, and it is
the one to steer by.

### 2.2 The guard, and it is not optional

**Sonnet must stay at or above 90% on the same suite.** The cheap way to lower any pass rate is to
make the prompt vague, and an ambiguous task measures nothing at all — it is noise dressed as
difficulty. Sonnet is the ceiling arm and the anti-ambiguity instrument: a task Sonnet fails is
under suspicion of being broken, not hard, until a reading proves otherwise.

So a desaturation round is only successful when **both** hold: Haiku down toward 80%, Sonnet still
at or above 90%. A round that moves Haiku by making Sonnet fail has failed and its tasks are
reverted.

Concretely, per task: **Sonnet 3/3, Haiku at most 2/3** is the shape being aimed for on most of
the suite. A task where Sonnet is 3/3 and Haiku is 0/3 is excellent and is kept. A task where both
fail is broken until proven otherwise.

### 2.3 The ladder — how to make a task harder without making it vague

In rough order of preference. The first three are the good ones.

1. **More material to hold at once.** The task requires reading and reconciling more real
   sources before the answer is decidable. This is the axis of interest (section 3) and hardening
   this way serves two goals at once.
2. **A trap that a plausible answer walks into.** t01 is the model: a sweep where two of four hits
   are correct history that "fixing" would damage. A model that acts on all of them looks
   productive and is wrong. Traps are how the confidently-wrong rate becomes measurable at all,
   and rev 5.9 section 7 makes that rate the headline. **At least half the comprehension variants
   must have a negative correct answer**: nothing found, claim false, leave it alone.
3. **More steps that must all be right.** Serial dependency, not more of the same in parallel — a
   task with six independent sub-answers is graded like six easy tasks; a task with three steps
   that each feed the next is genuinely harder.
4. **A tighter output contract**, but only where the contract is stated exactly and is checkable.
   Exact field order, exact separators, exact verbatim copying. This is legitimate but it measures
   instruction-following rather than comprehension, so it must not become the main lever.
5. **Longer wall or more output.** Weakest. See 2.4.

**Not on the ladder, ever:** ambiguity, missing information, unstated conventions, trick wording,
or anything the prompt does not say plainly. If two careful readers can disagree about what the
task asks, the task is broken.

### 2.4 The appetite measurement is itself evidence of easiness

The 2026-09-04 calibration measured, across six trials, output of **127 to 1,708 tokens against a
5,000-token ceiling**, and wall of **24.7 to 51.3 s against a 300 s target**. Those tasks are using
a fifth of their budget. That is a quantitative statement that the suite is small, and it is
consistent with the saturation.

Harder tasks should use materially more of that envelope. It is a symptom to steer by, not a
target to hit: a task that burns 280 s doing something trivial is not harder, it is slower. But a
suite whose hardest task finishes in 51 s is not testing much.

### 2.5 The iteration loop, which is the manager's and needs no permission

Repeat until the target in 2.1 is met, then keep going while time remains:

1. Pick the saturated tasks. Author harder variants — Luna authors, several candidates per task,
   the manager picks. Rev 5.9 section 4's authoring shape is unchanged.
2. Run the pair of cloud arms on the candidates: **Sonnet x3 and Haiku x3**. GLM stays available
   as a second reader but is not a gate.
3. Keep what moves Haiku down while Sonnet holds. Revert what moves Sonnet.
4. Record the round in `decisions.md`: which tasks changed, the before and after rates, and why
   each survivor survived.
5. Re-check the whole suite's aggregate against 2.1, and go again.

**Nothing in this loop is the owner's to approve.** Bank a question, keep authoring. Section 9.

### 2.6 The Haiku construction caveat is now smaller, and say so

Rev 5.9 section 7 carries a caveat that the Haiku comparison is circular because the suite is
built by discarding tasks Haiku passes. **Under this plan that is much less true**: the suite is
being tuned to a Haiku rate of about 80%, not to Haiku failing. Report Haiku's rate on the suite
as run, state the target it was tuned to, and drop the two-number reporting rev 5.9 required. The
honest sentence is now "the suite was sharpened until Haiku sat near 80%, and here is where the
quants land against that", which is a statement a reader can actually use.

## 3. Context comes from real material

### 3.1 What is withdrawn

`pibench.py --fill-tokens N` — prompt-side synthetic fill — is **withdrawn from scored runs**. It
was built on 2026-09-04, it works, and it is measured at 88-98% of target depending on which
denominator is used (see section 8). It is retired because it answers the wrong question. Filler
that the task does not need measures a model's tolerance for padding; the owner's actual question
is how much *real* material can be handed over in one go.

Leave the code in place and leave it tested. It stays available for harness diagnostics, and it is
the reason the 2026-09-04 fill measurements are trustworthy. It is simply not how a scored cell
gets its context.

Sandbox padding on disk stays exactly where 2026-09-04 left it: **realism only, never sized to the
cell**. It was already demoted for the same reason — it never reached the context in the first
place.

### 3.2 What replaces it

**Every task declares its own natural size, and the axis is that size.** A task is authored with
the real material it genuinely requires — documents to reconcile, a tree to search, modules to
change — and the token cost of that material is a property of the task, measured once and
recorded in its manifest.

The grid stops being "every task at every context" and becomes **quant x context band, where a
band is populated by the tasks that genuinely need it**. Author into bands:

| band | task material | what it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| small | roughly 4k-8k | the floor: can the quant do the work at all |
| medium | roughly 12k-20k | one real document or a small tree |
| large | roughly 30k-45k | several sources that must be reconciled |

t03 already belongs to the medium band by construction — eight graded facts out of a 12k-token
document — and is the model for how the others should be built.

### 3.3 The working-margin rule, which the 64k result forced

**A cell's `num_ctx` must be at least 1.6x the task's material, and the material must never exceed
about 60% of the window.**

This is not a style preference. At the 64k acceptance run, roughly 57.9k of a 65,536-token window
was prompt, and **the agentic loop collapsed to a single turn** — there was no room left to work
in. A cell configured that way measures *less room to think*, not *more to read*, and the two are
different findings that would have been reported as one.

So the large band runs at 64k, the medium band at 32k or 48k, the small band at 24k. A task is
never run in a cell that cannot hold it plus its working margin, and a trial that comes back
single-turn at a size where multiple turns were expected is **flagged in the artifact**, because
that is the signature of this fault recurring.

### 3.4 What this costs, honestly

The old design gave a clean 15-cell grid with every task in every cell. This one does not: a task
appears only in the bands its size permits, so the grid is ragged and the per-cell task counts
differ. That is the price of the axis meaning something. Report per band, not per cell, and state
the task count behind every number.

It also means **the context curve is coarser** — three bands, not four cells — and that a
"bend" may not be locatable to a single context value. Say so in the report rather than
interpolating one.

## 4. Arms and the grid

Unchanged from rev 5.9 section 5 and its measured quant curves, which stand:

- **Local quants:** Q2_K_L, Q3_K_S, IQ3_M reach 64k; Q3_K_M stops at 48k; **Q3_K_L is excluded** by
  the 32k floor. Above ~14.2 GB resident a config is **fair-weather** and every such trial is
  labelled — idle VRAM on this desktop drifts 1.0-2.9 GB and reclaims without warning.
- **Reference rows:** Sonnet as ceiling, Haiku as comparison. No third reference row; Fable is
  ruled out.
- **GLM** shapes the suite as a second reader and is not scored.

Grid tags are `q27-<QUANT>-<N>k`, each baking its own `num_ctx` and `PARAMETER num_gpu 66`. All
fifteen exist and were verified tag by tag on 2026-09-04. **Verify the count with `ollama list`
before any run anyway** — this table has been wrong before, and five suffix-less base tags sit
beside them that bake `num_ctx 32768` and no `num_gpu`, which is the exact silent
mis-measurement the grid exists to avoid.

**KV cache: use `q8_0`, the desktop's own setting, and stop the `q4_0` ritual.** Measured
2026-09-04: both 64k models loaded at full `num_ctx` under q8_0 and under q4_0 gave *identical*
resident sizes (14.70 and 15.11 GB, both 100% GPU; nvidia-smi differed by 6-42 MiB). Both q8_0
cells **fit**, which falsifies the prediction that neither would. There is therefore no capacity
reason to prefer q4_0, and running on the machine's live setting removes a conditional from the
report. The separate direct-server quality comparison in `kv-probe-plan-2026-09-03.md` still
stands on its own and is unaffected.

## 5. Run order

The suite is not scoreable until section 2.1 is met, and getting there is the work. Run order is
therefore:

1. **Desaturation rounds** (section 2.5) until Haiku is near 80% with Sonnet at or above 90%.
   Cloud only, no GPU, no permission needed. This is the phase to protect.
2. **Re-band the suite** (section 3.2): measure each surviving task's real material size, assign
   it a band, and record the size in its manifest. Cheap, and it must happen before any GPU cell.
3. **Local calibration** on the re-banded suite, per rev 5.9 section 4a: does the task fit, does a
   reference solution land under the appetite ceiling, does a trial finish under 300 s, does the
   checker fire on a real transcript. **Calibration is not selection** — 4a is the integrity rule
   of this whole campaign and is unchanged. Never keep, drop, reword or reorder a task because of
   whether a quant *passed* it.
4. **Bend-finding pass**: every quant against every band it can hold, one trial per task. This is
   the run to protect if the night runs short.
5. **Three-trial passes** in the bands where the curve moves.
6. **Confirming the winner**: best quant on the evidence, three trials on every task with fewer.

Steps 4-6 replace the old queue in `schedule.md`, which is rewritten against this plan.

## 6. Verdict rule

Rev 5.9 section 7 stands in full and is not restated: task-level, two of three trials under the
900 s wall, class viable at three of four and marginal at two, **the confidently-wrong rate as a
verdict line of its own that outranks pass rate**, the context curve reported rather than just the
winning cell, and the 2-bit line reported whatever the ranking says.

Three amendments, all from this plan:

- **Report per band, not per cell** (section 3.4), with the task count behind every number.
- **The Haiku caveat is replaced** by the sentence in section 2.6.
- **Single-turn trials are flagged** (section 3.3) and a band whose trials collapsed to one turn is

## 8. Traps, each with its workaround as an instruction

Every one of these was paid for once already. Do the workaround; do not re-derive the trap.

- **A prompt over 32,767 characters cannot be passed as an argv on Windows.** It dies at spawn
  with `WinError 206` and writes `"runs": []`, which reads like a model failure and is not.
  **Write the prompt to a file and pass it with pi's `@file`.** An empty result set is a harness
  fault until proven otherwise.
- **`/api/generate` silently truncates prompts to half `num_ctx`.** No error, no warning. Do not
  use it for anything context-sensitive.
- **Chars per token on this suite is 4.664, measured — not 5.95.** The old constant was 28% high
  and would silently overflow a window. Never size a prompt from a character count without it.
- **Ollama's own model runner is also named `llama-server.exe`.** Before any direct-server run,
  unload Ollama with `keep_alive: 0` — it returns the card from 15,357 MiB to 1,230 MiB in about
  two seconds. Without that, the KV harness's cleanup barrier cannot tell Ollama's runner from its
  own child: it burns its full 180 s reporting a misleading VRAM message, and in the wrong ordering
  it would `taskkill /T /F` Ollama's runner.
- **`pgrep -f <pattern>` matches the waiting process itself.** A poll loop written that way never
  exits; it cost 43 minutes once and was then misread as a running benchmark. Match on a pid file
  or on `agent-run status`.
- **Read a finished run's `final.txt`, never the tail of its stdout.** The wrapper streams
  progress; the run directory holds the answer. A correct report has already been recorded as a
  failure this way once.
- **Do not `git add -A` while a worker holds the tree.** It sweeps in whatever the worker is
  mid-way through writing. Stage paths explicitly.
- **Residency alone does not prove GPU execution.** On Windows/WDDM an oversized allocation raises
  no OOM: it spills to system RAM while still reporting `100% GPU` and `offloaded 66/66`. Log the
  `ollama ps` split, the `nvidia-smi` peak **and** measured gen tok/s, and flag any trial well
  below its quant's known resident curve as thrashing.
- **State which denominator an achieved-fill percentage uses.** The 2026-09-04 acceptance figures
  are 97.6/93.3 and 90.4/90.4 against the *requested* fill, and 95.3/91.1 and 88.3/88.3 against the
  cell's `num_ctx`. Both are correct; quoting one as the other is not.

## 9. Standing rules for the manager

- **One model on the GPU at a time.** Every local trial logs the `ollama ps` processor split, the
  `nvidia-smi` peak and measured gen tok/s.
- **A timeout is a fail and is recorded as one.**
- **Every pi change stays bench-local** through `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`. Nothing under `~/.pi` or the
  deployed skill roots is touched.
- **Bank, do not block.** When a decision is genuinely the owner's, write it down and carry on with
  everything that does not depend on it. Stopping the whole session on a banked question is the
  expensive mistake and it looks responsible while it wastes the window. This plan is written so
  that almost nothing is blocking: desaturation, re-banding, calibration and the bend-finding pass
  are all the manager's.
- **This plan removes items from `ansible-slb/org/pending.md` as they are finished and never adds
  one.** An issue found on the way is written to a dated findings page.
- **Evidence, not assertion.** A checkbox that says "done" is worth nothing next to the command
  that proves it. The grid-tag row in `schedule.md` read "done" when three of fifteen existed.
