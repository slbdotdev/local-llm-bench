# KV cache quantisation at 64k, rerun — 2026-09-10

*The rerun `results/v5/kv-probe-plan-2026-09-03.md` specifies. Run by the v7 round-five manager
on the desktop's RTX 5080, four cells, one server at a time, on the Ollama-vendored
`llama-server.exe` directly and never through Ollama. Harness:
`results/v5/kv-probe/kvquality.py` and `run-sweep.sh`, both rebuilt for this run. Raw records:
`results/v5/kv-probe/kv-64k-{mrIQ3M,UDQ3KXL}-{q8_0,q4_0}.json` and their `.stdout.log`s;
watchdog log `results/v5/kv-probe/kv-64k-watch.log`.*

## The answer

> **Is q8_0 KV measurably better than q4_0 at 64k on the same model? No.**

On `q27-mrIQ3M-64k`, the one model where both cells completed, q8_0 scored **120 of 128 fields
(93.75%, Wilson 95% [0.882, 0.968])** against q4_0's **118 of 128 (92.19%, [0.862, 0.957])** — a
difference of **two fields out of 128**, with intervals overlapping over almost their whole
length. All-four-fields-per-needle was 28 of 32 against 27 of 32, a difference of one needle. The
depth buckets tell the same story: q8_0 90.9 / 95.0 / 95.5 early/middle/late, q4_0 86.4 / 95.0 /
95.5. Both differences sit inside the noise of a 32-needle probe, and the probe was **not**
saturated — the stop rule declined to fire at 93.75% against its 98%/90% thresholds, so it had
room to show a difference and did not.

**And on the second model the question does not arise, because q8_0 does not fit.** On
`q27-UDQ3KXL-64k` the q8_0 cell loaded to **15,315 MiB on a 16,303 MiB card**, took **30.7 s** to
load against q4_0's 6.6 s, and then answered **1 of 32 needles before hitting the 600 s safety
cap** — no generation rate, no prompt rate, `partial: true`. That is the plan's predicted WDDM
spill, arriving as a measured throughput collapse rather than an out-of-memory error, and the plan
says in terms to treat it as the measured answer to the capacity question rather than as a broken
run. The same model at q4_0 completed in the ordinary way at 14,568 MiB and scored the best result
of the whole sweep, **124 of 128 (96.88%)**.

## Two substitutions and one addition, all of them forced, all of them recorded

1. **The plan's two models no longer exist on this host.** `ollama list` holds 35 models and
   neither `q27-IQ3_M-64k` nor `q27-Q3_K_S-64k` is among them, and the 2026-09-03 Q3_K_S blob
   `sha256-ba0c5dee3026…` is not in the blob store either. The plan forbids guessing a blob from
   a short id and requires run-time resolution, so the original pair is unreachable, not merely
   inconvenient. The substitutes are the two 64k models of the same two classes that **are**
   present: `q27-mrIQ3M-64k` for the IQ3_M cell and `q27-UDQ3KXL-64k` for the Q3-K cell, both
   resolved at run time from `ollama show --modelfile`. **What is lost is continuity with the
   16k and 24k rows of 2026-09-03**, which were measured on the vanished Q3_K_S blob; the plan's
   own question — is q8_0 better than q4_0 at 64k *on the same model* — is answered on the same
   model twice regardless.
2. **The RAM watchdog was re-implemented for this host.** `results/gpu-tune/watch.sh` is written
   for Git Bash on Windows (it `cd`s to `/c/…` and shells out to `pwsh`) and cannot run from WSL,
   and the plan itself records that its `/api/ps` model count and `ollama*` RSS cannot see a
   directly launched `llama-server` anyway. A WSL-side watchdog with the same contract — VRAM and
   free host RAM every 10 s, ALERT marker under the same 8 GiB threshold, plus a Windows process
   count that *can* see `llama-server.exe` — ran for the whole sweep and is at
   `results/v5/kv-probe/kv-64k-watch.log`.
3. **Ollama is stopped by the sweep itself, after blob resolution and before the first cell.**
   `ollama show --modelfile` needs the model service running, and the plan requires the blob to
   be resolved at run time; the plan also forbids running that service beside the direct server.
   `run-sweep.sh` now resolves both blobs, then kills `ollama app.exe` and `ollama.exe` and
   verifies the drain (zero `ollama`/`llama-server` processes, VRAM back to idle) before cell 1.
   *Trap, measured today and now a comment in the script:* under Git Bash, MSYS path conversion
   rewrites `taskkill /F /IM` into `taskkill C:/F C:/IM`, the kill silently does nothing, and the
   drain loop reports the processes still there. `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` is required.

## The harness this run needed, and why the old one could not have answered

The 2026-09-03 probe scored 24/24 at 16k and 24k for **both** cache types, which is a ceiling and
not a finding: a one-field checksum lookup at those windows cannot fail. Per the plan the probe
was rebuilt: 1,200 look-alike records, 32 needles spread from 1% to 99% depth, each naming **two**
distinct records and asking **four** independently scorable fields, with same-prefix and
adjacent-index near-miss decoys that the question names and warns against. 128 scored fields per
cell, an all-four-per-needle count beside the field count, and early/middle/late depth buckets.
Scoring is one pure function shared by the live run and an offline fixture; the fixture feeds it a
known-good set and a deliberately degraded one (one wrong field, one near-miss field, one missing
field) and **fails the run unless the degraded set scores strictly under 100% and loses exactly
three fields and three needles**. It also asserts that a checksum copied off a near-miss record is
rejected and that an unlabelled six-character string scores zero. The fixture is the first gate in
`run-sweep.sh` and it ran clean before anything touched the GPU.

That harder probe is what made the run informative: it landed at 92-97% rather than 100%, so the
saturation stop rule declined to fire and all four cells ran. **The rule was declared before the
run and not adjusted after seeing the score.** Asymmetric K/V is rejected in argument validation,
as the plan directs.

The lifecycle was rebuilt too: one cleanup barrier that terminates the whole server tree, waits
for parent and children, waits for the port, and requires three consecutive samples that are
simultaneously VRAM-quiet and free of any `llama-server` process; a unique port per launch,
re-verified unbound immediately before `Popen`; kill-and-drain before a health failure is
recorded; and the server-log tail in every startup error. Every barrier in this run reported
`ok=True` in 4.3 to 5.8 s, including after the spilled cell.

## What the org should adopt

`OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE` is one environment variable on the Ollama server: not per-model, not
per-request. The plan asks which of three shapes follows. **It is the first: a single global
setting, at `q4_0`.**

* q8_0 buys nothing measurable in quality at 64k — two fields in 128 on the one model where both
  cells ran, well inside the interval.
* q8_0 costs about **960 MiB** of VRAM at 64k (15,326 against 14,366 MiB on mrIQ3M; 15,315 against
  14,568 on UDQ3KXL, the latter a load-time figure), and about **24 s** of extra load time on both
  models, which is the allocator working near the ceiling.
* On the larger of the two 64k models that cost is the difference between a working server and an
  unusable one: 1 needle in 600 s.

The desktop's current setting is `q8_0` (logged by the sweep and left unchanged, as the plan
requires — cache types are per-server flags on the direct launch, so nothing was modified). Moving
it to `q4_0` costs no measured quality at 64k, returns about a gigabyte of VRAM, and removes the
one configuration in this sweep that failed outright. **No documented switch and no restart
discipline is needed, which was the point of asking.**

One caveat to carry: this is 32 needles on two models on one card, and the difference it can
resolve is a few percent. It rules out a *large* q8_0 advantage at 64k; it does not rule out a
small one. What it establishes without qualification is the capacity result, and on this card the
capacity result is the decision.
