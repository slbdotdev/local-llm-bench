## The mission, short

Find the best quant for the work this 27B might actually be good at on the RTX
5080. Quant determines reachable context; the question is how quality holds as
context fills from 24k to 64k, and whether the quants needed to reach the long
windows destroy quality getting there. The realistic role is the worker that
still exists when the paid windows are gone. **The confidently-wrong rate is
the headline instrument, not the pass rate** — a worker that fails visibly
costs a retry, while one that is fluent and wrong costs the verification the
delegation was meant to save.

## The lines you do not cross

- **Section 4a is law: calibration yes, selection never.** Size a task with
  local runs — window fit, appetite, wall, checker firing, prompt clarity.
  Never keep, drop, reword or reorder a task because a quant *passed* it.
  Learning what the local model can and cannot do is the **result**, not an
  input. Record per task which local runs informed it and which property they
  settled, so the selection can be audited rather than trusted.
- **Bank, do not block.** Ambiguity goes in the decision log and you carry on
  with everything that does not depend on it. Never block waiting on a human,
  and never take a structural decision alone — dropping a task class, moving a
  verdict arity, or changing what a class measures all get banked.
- **The GPU must be idle, not merely serial.** One open Unity editor takes
  ~4 GB and leaves 11.9 GB free, less than every quant under test needs. Close
  editors first; a trial taken with one open is void, not merely suspect. One
  model on the card at a time. Every trial logs the `ollama ps` split,
  `nvidia-smi` peak memory **and measured gen tok/s** — judge residency by
  tok/s, never by a reported `100% GPU`, which on Windows/WDDM lies while the
  allocation silently spills to system RAM.
- The grid pins `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q4_0`; the desktop's live setting is
  `q8_0`. Set it, and **revert it and restart Ollama when you are done.**
- Every pi change stays bench-local through `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`. Nothing
  under `~/.pi` or the deployed skill roots.
- A timeout is a fail, recorded as one. **8 hours is the goal, 12 the hard
  limit.** Stop early on repeated harness failure rather than burning the
  window on a wedged card.

## The worker runtime, as of this handoff

You are its first real consumer. It was acceptance-tested on 2026-09-04 and
four defects were found and fixed; the evidence page is
`ansible-slb/org/worker-runtime-acceptance-2026-09-04.md` and it is worth ten
minutes of your time, because its method is the one to copy: give a worker a
task whose answer you can compute independently, and size the task to the race
you are testing.

What is verified working, live: run ids and durable state under
`~/.agent-runs/<id>/`; `agent-run list|status|result|wait`; **live messaging to
both harnesses** (`agent-msg <run-id> "text"`, which had never once worked for
Codex before that day); `--notify <name@host>` delivering a terminal state and
the full answer to an inbox; the `AGENT_RUN_DEPTH` refusal at all three
entrypoints. Run every delegated worker with `run_in_background: true` — that
is what gives you both the completion notification and the answer.

**Never sit in a foreground `agent-run wait` while you have other work, which
as manager you always do.** You will be running several Luna authoring runs at
once against a serial GPU queue; blocking on any one of them throws away the
parallelism that shape exists to buy, and a foreground wait looks exactly like
a hang while it parks you for its whole `--timeout`. Launch, move on, answer
the notification; `agent-run status` reads a run's state without blocking. The
rule is canonical in the `agent-runtime` skill.

Two things to know when a worker misbehaves. A pi run's transcript is **not**
in `harness.log` — pi runs in RPC mode over a supervisor-owned pipe, so
`harness.log` holds only stderr, and the real diagnosis is in
`<run-dir>/pi-session/*.jsonl`. And an upstream provider error mid-stream is a
real failure mode: one was measured that day (Together, on GLM 5.3 Flash).
Plan section 3 rule 8 — endpoint failures are not model failures — is why that
distinction is worth making carefully in your gate records.

  commit anyone else's changes with yours.

  never yourself as a contributor. Do not push, and never run a converge or an
